## The whole-book question

Did Frank Sheeran kill Jimmy Hoffa — and if so, how does his full life (Depression, WWII, Teamsters, La Cosa Nostra under Russell Bufalino) make that crime intelligible as the end of a continuous moral and institutional story rather than a one-off mystery?

## Unity statement

Brandt reconstructs twentieth-century American organized crime, labor power, and the Hoffa disappearance through hundreds of hours of interviews with Frank “The Irishman” Sheeran, who on his deathbed stands behind the book’s account of painting Hoffa’s “house.” The narrative arc is Sheeran’s formation as a killer (war → trucking → Bufalino → Hoffa) and the collision of loyalty to two “greatest men,” ending in the July 30, 1975 hit and decades of silence. The book argues that the famous unsolved crime is solved by confession, context, and corroborating details, to be judged in the “court of public opinion.”
