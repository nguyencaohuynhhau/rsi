# ch09: Ch27–29: July 30, 1975 — To Paint a House (pp. 196–210)

## Question this chapter answers

What, on Sheeran’s account, happened logistically and physically on July 30, 1975 — how Hoffa was lured, who was in the house, how the shooting was done, what became of the body — and what personal and mob aftermath followed in the days after the disappearance?

## Leading propositions

- Hoffa refused to stop his 1976 comeback; he claimed secret records that would “rock this nation” if he were harmed, and answered the death warning with “They wouldn’t dare.” (pp. 196–197)
- Sheeran reported Hoffa’s intent and the records threat back to Russell Bufalino; Russell called Hoffa’s thinking “distorted” and could not understand why Hoffa “just doesn’t go away.” (p. 196)
- The hit was built around Bill Bufalino’s daughter’s wedding (Aug. 1, 1975), which made it plausible that Tony Pro and Russell would be near Detroit for a sit-down. (p. 198)
- The Tony Pro pension beef was a decoy; the real purpose was to get Hoffa out to a meeting arranged by Tony Giacalone for 2:30 at the Machus Red Fox. (p. 198)
- Sheeran was put in the hit because the alternative was killing him; participation proved he had not been freelancing against Pro/Fitz for Hoffa. (pp. 199–200)
- Alibi architecture: wives left at a Port Clinton restaurant; Sheeran flies Port Clinton → Pontiac (~1 hour) while a car trip Detroit would take ~3 hours one way, so women never suspect the Detroit errand. (pp. 199–200)
- Loaner gray Ford at Pontiac; directions: Telegraph Rd past Red Fox, left on Seven Mile, past bridges, house with brown shingles, high backyard fence, detached garage. (pp. 200–201)
- Body disposal must stay local: Sheeran rejects 55-gallon drum / Giants Stadium / New Jersey dump theories as operationally insane for a high-profile corpse. (p. 201)
- A car hit is “crazy” because death odor permanently ruins the vehicle; a house does not retain the death smell the same way. (pp. 201–202)
- Personnel at the house: Sally Bugs (Briguglio) in vestibule; two young Italian “Pro’s people” (one Andretta brother) as cleaners in kitchen; Chuckie O’Brien as innocent-dupe driver of Tony Jack’s son’s maroon Mercury. (pp. 202–204)
- Chuckie is presented as unknowing bait: braggart, untrustworthy with secrets, kept ignorant so he would act normal with “Dad.” (pp. 203–204)
- Psychology of the lure: Jimmy waits from 2:00 (for Sheeran) through 2:30 (for the Tonys) plus ~15 minutes; rage then embarrassment + sight of Sheeran + claim Russell is waiting make him cooperative. (pp. 205–206)
- Hoffa hair DNA later found in the Mercury trunk is, on Sheeran’s account, irrelevant: “Jimmy was never in the trunk, dead or alive.” (p. 205)
- Shooting: two shots “at a decent range—not too close or the paint splatters back at you—in the back of the head behind his right ear.” (p. 207)
- Post-hit: Sheeran drops the piece on linoleum, drives to Pontiac airport, flies back to Port Clinton; cleaners bag body, trunk of brown Buick, cremation; Russell’s only comment is a wink about a “pleasant flight.” (pp. 207–208)
- Aug. 4, 1975 Vesuvio meeting: report to Fat Tony Salerno; New York did not sanction it, but did not oppose it either; Detroit (and Chicago) sanction required. (p. 208)
- Tony Pro wants Sheeran dead for alleged knowledge of Hoffa’s plot against him; Russell has Sheeran deny it; Fat Tony accepts the denial. (pp. 208–209)
- Body disposal detail is uncertain even to Sheeran: Russell said funeral parlor (possibly Anthony Bagnasco, Grosse Pointe Shores) not Pete Vitale’s incinerator (“too obvious”); Sheeran allows Russell may have misdirected him. (pp. 209–210)
- Family cost: daughter Peggy, Aug. 3, 1975: “I don’t even want to know a person like you”; permanent estrangement. (p. 210)

## Arguments

- **Premises**: Hoffa would not stand down, claimed catastrophic records, and believed he was untouchable (“They wouldn’t dare”); Russell and “our friends” needed him gone before a 1976 fight that threatened the status quo. **Conclusion**: The hit became operationally inevitable once Hoffa refused to “go away.”
- **Premises**: Sheeran was Hoffa’s trusted Irishman and Russell’s man; if excluded from the hit he would be a liability; if included he could never flip without confessing his own role. **Conclusion**: Putting Sheeran in “the thing” both uses and neutralizes him.
- **Premises**: Public restaurant meeting (Red Fox) plus familiar faces (Chuckie, Sheeran) plus implied presence of Russell (private sit-down man) plus maroon Mercury of Tony Jack’s son. **Conclusion**: The psychology of the bait overrides Hoffa’s street sense and gets him into a private house in daylight.
- **Premises**: High-profile body; time pressure (Tony Jack’s alibi window; Sheeran’s reconnection with wives in Ohio); smell/trace problems of cars and long transport. **Conclusion**: Local house kill + local cremation is the only rational disposal plan; far-flung drum/stadium theories are outsider fantasy.
- **Premises**: New York’s non-opposition + Detroit/Chicago territorial sanction + five-day Vesuvio report to Fat Tony. **Conclusion**: The hit was a multi-family political act, not a freelanced personal vendetta by Pro alone — though Pro’s people executed key roles.
- **Premises**: Sheeran did not personally dispose of the body; Russell gave him a funeral-parlor account that may have been misdirection. **Conclusion**: On the confession’s own terms, cremation method and exact site are the weakest, least first-hand link in the chain.

## Evidence offered

**A. Sheeran’s first-person operational narrative (confession core — not independently verified in this unit)**

- Timeline structure: Port Clinton grass strip → plane with averted-gaze pilot → Pontiac airfield (no flight plan/records) → gray Ford loaner → past Red Fox on Telegraph → Seven Mile / bridges / brown-shingle house. (pp. 199–201)
- On-scene cast and roles: Sally Bugs (watch Sheeran/Chuckie), two kitchen cleaners, Chuckie as dupe, Sheeran as trusted backup who is actually the shooter. (pp. 202–207)
- Parking-lot confrontation dialogue: Jimmy’s rage at Chuckie and Sally Bugs; Sally points to Sheeran; “His friend wanted to be at the thing. They’re at the house waitin’.” (pp. 204–205)
- Vestibule mechanics: Jimmy walks ahead; Sheeran closes behind “the way you get right behind a prisoner”; empty house = recognition of the hit; Jimmy tries the door. (p. 207)
- Immediate exit protocol: drop gun on linoleum, head down to loaner, back to Pontiac, pilot again looks away; Detroit operation timed to ~one hour. (p. 207)
- Russell’s second-hand disposal briefing: body bag, back door, Buick trunk, cremation; cleaners pick up Sally at Pete Vitale’s, fly back to Jersey to report to Tony Pro. (p. 207)

**B. Documentary / chronological scaffolding (Brandt’s or public-record framing mixed with Sheeran)**

- April 1975 Teamsters-convention rumor that Hoffa was cooperating with FBI; Detroit Free Press (Dec. 20, 1992) attributes rumor to Chuckie O’Brien; HOFFEX 302 notes possible government trade for lifting union restrictions. (p. 197)
- May 15, 1975: Hoffa Fifth at grand jury on Local 299 “no show” jobs; same day meeting with son and Tony Jack; refuses Pro meeting and Giacalone’s records request. (p. 197)
- Late May: Fitzsimmons threatens Local 326 trusteeship. (pp. 197–198)
- June 19, 1975: Sam Giancana assassinated before Church Committee testimony. (p. 198)
- June 25: Ralph Proctor (Fitzsimmons camp) beaten; July 10: Richard Fitzsimmons’s Lincoln bombed at Nemo’s. (p. 198)
- July 30: Hoffa disappears after being seen in Red Fox parking lot. (p. 198)
- Aug. 4 Vesuvio attendees noted by FBI: Fat Tony Salerno, Russell Bufalino, Frank Sheeran, Tony Pro, Sally Bugs. (p. 208)
- FBI checked Anthony Bagnasco funeral parlor, Grosse Pointe Shores (Sheeran says he read this during the investigation). (p. 209)

**C. What is *not* claimed as Sheeran’s direct knowledge**

- Exact final cremation site: funeral parlor vs. Vitale incinerator left unresolved; “this detail was none of my business.” (pp. 209–210)
- Whether Tony Pro / Fat Tony had final say on disappearing the body in a way that hurt the family: “That we’ll never know for sure.” (p. 197)

## Tensions / links to other chapters

- **Closes ch08’s collision course**: “They wouldn’t dare” (end of Philly confrontation) is the immediate premise of July 30 logistics; reporting the records threat to Russell converts personal warning into institutional decision.
- **Activates ch05 “paint houses”**: The code phrase becomes literal operational sequence — house, paint (blood), cleaners, body bag.
- **Dual loyalty climax (ch05–ch08)**: Sheeran is simultaneously Hoffa’s “backup” and Russell’s instrument; the vestibule shot is the structural resolution of serving two “greatest men.”
- **Sets up ch10 investigation**: Cast list here (Pro, Andrettas, Sally Bugs, Sheeran, Bufalino, Giacalone, Chuckie) is exactly the HOFFEX short list pursued for decades.
- **Disposal tension for ch10/epilogue**: Sheeran’s “none of my business” on cremation vs. later public obsession with Giants Stadium / drum theories; local-cremation claim remains first-hand-weak.
- **Moral aftermath prefigures vow-of-secrecy unit**: Peggy’s rejection (Aug. 3) and heavy later drinking (ch10) mark private cost of stand-up silence.
- **Corpse-car vs. hair-in-trunk**: Sheeran’s insistence Jimmy was never in the trunk will later be set against FBI DNA from the Mercury (rear headrest hair in epilogue — distinct from trunk claim here).

## Open questions

- How much of the pre-July timeline (Giancana, Proctor beating, Lincoln bombing) is causal setup versus atmospheric coincidence in Brandt’s narrative?
- Was Chuckie truly ignorant, or is “innocent bystander” Sheeran’s protective construction (he repeatedly says he feels sorry for Chuckie)?
- Which Andretta brother was present; who is the unnamed second “kid”; can alibis be tested against this timeline?
- Did Russell intentionally misdirect Sheeran on Vitale’s incinerator vs. funeral parlor — and does that undercut confidence in *all* second-hand disposal details?
- Why permanent disappearance rather than a body recovered in a way that allowed a funeral — Pro’s cruelty, Fat Tony’s order, or operational necessity of total silence?
- Can the brown-shingle house, bridges, and footbridge directions be fixed to a real address independently of Sheeran (addressed in afterword/epilogue of next unit)?
- What exactly did Hoffa’s “records” contain, and did anyone ever verify they existed outside Sheeran's report of Hoffa’s talk?
