# ch05: Jimmy / “I Heard You Paint Houses” / They Didn’t Make a Parachute Big Enough / The Gunman Had No Mask (pp. 70–98)

## Question this chapter answers

How does Frank Sheeran meet Jimmy Hoffa, what does “I heard you paint houses” mean as labor–mob code, and how do early enforcement jobs lock him into dual loyalty to Hoffa and Bufalino?

## Leading propositions

- Jimmy Hoffa was, in his heyday, as famous as Elvis and then the Beatles; later fame is mostly the disappearance (p. 70).
- Labor–management warfare in Hoffa’s rise used goons on both sides; unions also warred with rival unions and crushed internal reformers (pp. 72–73).
- Hoffa’s “ends” (worker gains) justified any “means,” including alliance with the national crime syndicate (pp. 74–75).
- The first words Hoffa spoke to Sheeran were “I heard you paint houses,” sealing a hire into International-level enforcement (p. 83).
- The Central States Pension Fund became “the goose that laid the golden eggs” for La Cosa Nostra loans and Hoffa’s off-books points (pp. 84–85).
- Hoffa fed Beck to the McClellan Committee—behavior Brandt flags as “ratting” that later godfathers would not forget (pp. 86–87).
- Sheeran’s first named Chicago “straightening” leaves the body on the sidewalk as a message; masks in newspaper accounts are usually eyewitness fiction (pp. 93–94).
- Sheeran claims Hoffa knew Jack Ruby via Giancana and Red Dorfman, and overheard Giancana–Hoffa debate over fixing Illinois for JFK (pp. 96–97).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: (1) Management hired Sicilian strikebreakers; cops looked away. (2) Hoffa was beaten repeatedly as Local 299 agent. (3) Unions used bombings, arsons, beatings, murders. **Conclusion**: Hoffa’s mob “resistance” is presented as reciprocal warfare, not pure corruption—Brandt’s framing echoes Hoffa’s TV line that employers hire hoodlums first (pp. 72–75).
- **Premises**: Sheeran already enforces loans, hangs with Skinny Razor, survives a jewelry-deadbeat homicide investigation; Russell and Angelo vouch him. **Conclusion**: The phone interview is not a random discovery of talent but formalization of an already-made killer into Hoffa’s service (pp. 77–83).
- **Premises**: Brand-new unfired gun in apartment; prints explainable by borrowing a crib; alibi-building at Haverford Diner. **Conclusion** (Sheeran): Bosses send a friend to kill so evidence has “innocent explanations”; he then maps this pattern onto the Jimmy Hoffa hair-in-the-car problem (pp. 79–80).
- **Premises**: Cheasty sting photos; air-tight case; jury of eight Blacks; Joe Louis hug; NAACP seed planted; toy parachute to RFK. **Conclusion** (Brandt): Hoffa beat the system with race theater and star power—and learned he could never lose (pp. 87–88).
- **Premises**: Organizing work is cover; Bill Isabel “heard you paint houses”; Chicago job set up with crash cars and piece-disposal. **Conclusion**: Union “public relations” and picket work are the legal face of a killing machine (pp. 90–94).

## Evidence offered

**Brandt framing / public-record material**

- Strike statistics post-WWII; Strawberry Boys story; Henry Ford anti-union quote (pp. 70–72).
- Victor Riesel acid attack ordered by Johnny Dio; McClellan Committee; RFK as interrogator (pp. 72–73).
- Wiretap quote attributed to Hoffa on Riesel: acid on the hands he types with (p. 73)—sourced as insider knowledge, not always footnoted.
- Bobby Kennedy / McClellan quotes on Teamsters as “conspiracy of evil” and “superpower” (pp. 74–75).
- Budd Schulberg on stink-bomb / Teamster-driver threats that killed *The Enemy Within* film; Bill Bufalino letter to Columbia (pp. 75–76).
- Sheridan on Hoffa feeding Beck’s lawyer to Kennedy—Brandt treats as ethical bombshell and as later “rat” label among bosses (p. 86).
- Cheasty bribery arrest, 1957 trial, Joe Louis stunt, acquittal, parachute gift (pp. 87–88).
- Provenzano rise; Castellito murder by Konigsberg, Sinno, Briguglio (p. 89).
- Wiretap “fruit of the poisonous tree” kills perjury case (p. 90).
- Moldea tape-recording young James P. Hoffa confirming father’s relationship with Jack Ruby (p. 96).
- History Channel-style claim that Giancana fixed Illinois for Kennedy (p. 97)—asserted, not independently documented in the text.

**Sheeran oral testimony (voice)**

- Self-exile from home after Whispers; room near Skinny Razor; “all the way down” (p. 70).
- Robber shot in both kneecaps; new-gun hygiene (pp. 77–78).
- Deadbeat with “dying mother” story; bar fight at Harry Riccobene’s (pp. 78–79).
- Jewelry-welsher basement murder investigation; fingerprint on banister (pp. 79–80).
- Thirty-nine of forty-four Golden Lantern waitresses; Dante’s Inferno muscle for Lopinson/Malito (pp. 81–82).
- Phone call: “I heard you paint houses” / stammered carpentry reply; ticket and $100s (pp. 83–84).
- Pension-fund loans, Dorfmans, Jack Ruby as officer of Waste Handlers (pp. 84–85)—hearsay nested inside oral history.
- Detroit taxi drive; Bill Isabel dynamite reputation; “rebel factions are like Nazi collaborators” (pp. 90–92).
- Chicago sidewalk hit setup; refuses envelope (“I’ll do a friend a favor”) (pp. 93–94).
- Coke bottles off a bridge as non-lethal freight-hauler pressure (p. 95).
- Suite life with Hoffa; Giancana and Ruby visits; Cuba rage with Russell; Frank Chavez’s Oswald-flowers letter (pp. 95–98).

**Evidence quality notes (history genre)**

- Strongest when Brandt cites public events (Riesel, McClellan, Cheasty photos, AFL-CIO expulsion, Apalachin timing).
- Weakest when Sheeran asserts sole-perpetrator hits with “don’t ask me who or what” and when high-level plots (election fix, Ruby–Oswald chain) rest on overheard suite talk decades later.
- Self-serving alibi pattern: every near-charge (jewelry murder, later Dante’s) resolves with someone else taking the fall or no charge—reader must weigh stand-up code vs. fabrication.

## Tensions / links to other chapters

- **Links back (ch04)**: Russell’s patronage and “Irishman” nickname culminate in the Hoffa introduction; Russell loans Sheeran to Angelo for training, then gifts him to Hoffa.
- **Links forward (ch06–ch09)**: “Paint houses” becomes the through-line to July 30, 1975; pension-fund “goose” and Tony Pro’s rise prefigure later Commission–Hoffa war; hair-in-car explanation here pre-defends forensic doubt about the disappearance car.
- **Voice vs. framing**: Brandt’s historical essays (ch. 11, 13) lionize and indict Hoffa with secondary sources; Sheeran’s chapters (12, 14) supply the killer’s apprenticeship. The book blurs them so Hoffa’s public myth and private hire feel continuous.
- **Moral tension**: Sheeran calls leaving Mary and the girls “the worst mistake I made in my life” (p. 70) while celebrating ego, waitresses, and hits—Catholic conscience appears only as retrospective caption.
- **Hoffa as rat**: Brandt weaponizes Sheridan’s Beck sentence against Hoffa’s later standing with Bufalino et al.—a thesis that will matter when Hoffa threatens to expose the fund under Fitzsimmons.

## Open questions

- How much of the jewelry-basement murder and the unnamed Chicago sidewalk hit is confessional truth versus generic tradecraft folklore?
- Did Hoffa actually utter “paint houses” on first contact, or is the line a retrospective myth that organizes the book’s title?
- How reliable is Sheeran’s Ruby–Giancana–Hoffa suite network given Moldea’s separate young-Hoffa confirmation covers acquaintance, not conspiracy?
- To what degree did Hoffa control Dorfman loans versus being a partner who took “just a taste”?
- Is the friend-as-shooter evidence theory applied to Hoffa’s hair scientifically plausible or only rhetorical inoculation?
