# ch04: Russell Bufalino and All the Way Downtown (pp. 51–69)

## Question this chapter answers

How did Russell Bufalino’s patronage—and one near-fatal “mistake” downtown—complete Frank Sheeran’s transformation from restless Teamster hustler into a trusted, lethal enforcer under Philadelphia and national mob authority?

## Leading propositions

- Domestic neglect and “another culture” begin when Sheeran hangs downtown with Italian Food Fair drivers; he still brings cash home weekly but stops being present; Teamster cover means firing only if theft is proven. (p. 51)
- 1957 (Apalachin + McClellan televised hearings) forces public recognition of a national Mafia; Hoover’s denial collapses. (pp. 51–52)
- Russell Bufalino is a national-level power: Commission influence, deportation fights, casino/Cuba interests, alleged CIA-Castro plot, Pennsylvania territory, quiet style. (pp. 52–54)
- Meeting Bufalino (Endicott truck-stop engine help, mid-1950s) “changed my life” second only to marriage and daughters after the war. (p. 54)
- Downtown apprenticeship: football lottery for Joey McGreal, loansharking for Skinny Razor, systematic Food Fair meat theft via palm-the-seal method. (pp. 55–58)
- Sheeran’s refusal to rat in the 1956 interstate-theft case and later resignation to save a co-conspirator certify him as “stand-up.” (pp. 58, 60)
- Recognition of Bufalino’s real stature comes only after Sheeran drives him to Apalachin and reads the raid coverage. (pp. 62–63)
- Whispers DiTullio hires Sheeran for $10,000 to destroy Cadillac Linen Service (Bruno/Jewish-partner property) while insisting on secrecy—a setup. (pp. 64–66)
- Bufalino intercedes; Bruno spares Sheeran on condition he kill Whispers by morning—Sheeran’s first fully narrated mob hit. (pp. 67–69)
- Sheeran concludes he “I owed that man my life”—and that it was only the first time. (p. 69)

## Arguments

- **Premises**: (1) Postwar Sheeran already steals, fights, and hustles under Teamster cover; (2) downtown offers higher-yield crime and status; (3) “stand-up” conduct is the admission ticket. **Conclusion**: The mob does not invent Sheeran’s dishonesty; it selects and grades him for loyalty under pressure.
- **Premises**: Bufalino appears first as a humble mechanic/helper; gifts of bread and sausage build intimacy; being seen with him confers protection and status. **Conclusion**: Bufalino’s power works through personal sponsorship and visibility, not through Sheeran understanding the org chart up front.
- **Premises**: Whispers hides Bruno’s piece of Cadillac, pays strong money, forbids consultation with Skinny Razor, and leaves Sheeran as the only identifiable “giant” casing the plant. **Conclusion**: The Cadillac job is a jealousy hit-by-proxy on Sheeran’s rising proximity to Bufalino; the real test is whether Sheeran will execute the sanctioned counter-hit.
- **Premises**: Sheeran maps Bruno’s order onto wartime prisoner disposal (“hurry back”); he uses a .32, reads the paper for an hour (“That could have been me”), then receives drinks and dinner as respect. **Conclusion**: The enforcer identity is sealed when combat ethics and mob ethics become the same sentence—“you gotta do what you gotta do”—under Bufalino’s protection.
- **Premises**: Brandt supplies Apalachin, McClellan, Justice/deportation, Time/CIA-Castro, Pennsylvania Crime Commission, and McClellan’s “ruthless and powerful” label for Bufalino. **Conclusion**: The book argues Bufalino is not a local character actor but a structural boss whose friendship explains Sheeran’s survival and later national usefulness (Hoffa path still ahead).

## Evidence offered

### Brandt’s historical frame (ch. 8)
- Apalachin raid: ~58 seized, ~50 escaped; meeting after Anastasia barbershop murder (Oct. 1957). (pp. 51–52)
- McClellan hearings’ televised Fifth Amendment culture. (p. 52)
- Bufalino biography: born Rosario Bufalino, Sicily, 1903; near-deportation with Carlos Marcello; Marcello’s Guatemala episode; 1980 PA Crime Commission claim of expanded control; Medico Industries silent partnership; Batista-era Cuba holdings lost to Castro; Time (June 1975) / Church Committee on CIA-gangland Castro plot; three 1970s acquittals, one five days before Hoffa disappeared. (pp. 52–53)
- Territory and Carrie Sciandra family ties; friendship with Angelo Bruno (“quiet Don” / “Docile Don”); contrast of Bruno family’s post-1980 chaos with Bufalino’s stable control until death (1994, age 90). (pp. 53–54)
- McClellan Committee characterization: “one of the most ruthless and powerful leaders of the Mafia in the United States.” (p. 54)
- Anecdote: retired police chief of Bufalino’s town—polite “old-school” gentleman who kept crime out of local jurisdiction. (p. 54)
- Sheeran’s comparison: Brando’s *Godfather* mannerisms closest to Bufalino among bosses he met. (p. 54)

### Sheeran’s oral narrative (ch. 9–10)
- Food Fair refrigerator truck, Endicott breakdown, Bufalino starts the engine—“Can I give you a hand, kiddo?” (p. 54)
- Bocce Club, Friendly Lounge, Skinny Razor loanshark intro, McGreal football lottery. (pp. 55–56)
- Seal-palming hindquarter theft scheme; whole-load Atlantic City “empty truck” story; Nov. 5, 1956 indictment; stolen shortage records; case thrown out; $25,000 resignation offer refused. (pp. 56–58)
- Villa d’Roma: reunion with Bufalino + Angelo Bruno; prosciutto bread / Roselli’s sausages; Catania war talk. (pp. 58–59)
- Second Food Fair pressure: Globe Detective catches drop man; Sheeran resigns to save him. (p. 60)
- First “errands”: Eddie Rece Jersey girlfriend matter (gun shown not used); Atlantic City vig collection for Skinny. (pp. 60–61)
- Nickname Cheech; Messina Club access; Wednesday dinners with Carrie and Mary; driving Bufalino (including drop-off at Apalachin). (pp. 61–62)
- Deportation help later (lawyer via Italian government)—gratitude bond. (p. 63)
- Whispers Melrose Diner pitch: $2,000 down, $8,000 on success, solo arson/bombing of Cadillac Linen, secrecy from Skinny. (pp. 63–66)
- Surveilled casing; Friendly Lounge confrontation; Villa d’Roma “court-martial” with door locked. (pp. 66–67)
- Whispers found shot on sidewalk with small-caliber (.32); Sheeran’s gun from Eddie Rece goes missing. (pp. 68–69)
- Aftermath: respect at Friendly; dinner with Bruno; introduction to Cappy Hoffman and Woody Weisman (Jewish Cadillac partners). (p. 69)

### Scrutinizing the evidence
- **Bufalino dossier (ch. 8)** is almost entirely Brandt’s research voice—commission reports, press, Senate—while Sheeran supplies the personal feel (handshake, wine, bread). The CIA-Castro and Medico claims are secondary-source assertions in the book, not proven here by Sheeran.
- **Apalachin drive**: Sheeran says he dropped Bufalino at “this house” and “didn’t see anything unusual”—compatible with courier ignorance; also convenient for limiting his legal exposure in the telling.
- **Food Fair case**: Sheeran’s account of anonymous records and stand-up witnesses is self-serving oral history; the thrown-out case and resignation are plausible but not independently documented in this chapter.
- **Whispers murder**: Classic uncorroborated confession structure—newspaper “unknown assailant,” no witnesses, missing .32. Motive chain (jealousy of Bufalino access) is Sheeran/Bufalino’s interpretation as reported by Sheeran. Date is “next morning” after Bruno’s deadline, not a calendar date.
- **“Higher than a made man”**: Status claim rests on Bufalino’s alleged words (“Nobody can ever touch you because you are with me”)—powerful in narrative, unverifiable here.
- **Voice split**: Ch. 8 is author-historian; ch. 9–10 return to long Sheeran monologue with Brandt’s earlier scaffolding making the names (Bruno, Apalachin) legible.

## Tensions / links to other chapters

- **Back to war chapters (4–7)**: Explicit equating of Bruno’s hit order with prisoner-disposal orders; Whispers’s “you were in the war” pitch recruits combat competence; Sheeran’s sensory hyper-alertness at Villa d’Roma likened to a landing craft. Fatalism and “encased in lead” make the sidewalk hit thinkable.
- **Back to early life**: “Growing up around my old man, I knew when something was wrong” at the locked-door meeting—family violence as threat detection.
- **Forward to Hoffa**: Stand-up reputation, Bufalino sponsorship, and proven willingness to kill on order are the résumé Hoffa will need; labor racketeering is already listed among national mob businesses Sheeran reads about after Apalachin. Food Fair/Teamsters skill set remains the legitimate cover.
- **Marriage plot**: Mary receives cash (including $1,500 of Whispers’s down payment as “hit the number”) while Sheeran disappears into downtown—economic provision without presence; second marriage/Connie mentioned only as later contrast when “already in position” with Hoffa.
- **Brandt vs. Sheeran on Bufalino**: Brandt’s “ruthless” Commission boss vs. Sheeran’s wine-dunking mentor and life-saver; the book needs both—structural power and intimate debt.
- **Irish outsider / Italian inside**: Cannot be “made,” yet claims protection above made men—central identity tension for the rest of the book.

## Open questions

- What independent evidence exists for Sheeran’s role in the Whispers killing beyond this confession and the missing-gun detail?
- How soon after Apalachin did Bufalino resume using Sheeran as a driver, and how much of the “avoiding publicity” gap is memory compression?
- Is the jealousy motive (Whispers resenting Sheeran’s access to Bufalino) Bufalino’s self-serving frame that Sheeran accepts?
- How should readers weigh Brandt’s CIA–Castro–Bufalino material in a chapter that otherwise prepares a local Philly hit story?
- When Sheeran says he was “higher up than a made man,” is that operational truth, flattery remembered as fact, or narrative positioning for later Hoffa claims?
