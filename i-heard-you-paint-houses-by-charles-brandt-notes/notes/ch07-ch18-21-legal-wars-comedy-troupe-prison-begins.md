# ch07: Legal wars, Comedy Troupe, prison begins (pp. 126–155)

## Question this chapter answers

How did the RFK / “Get Hoffa” legal campaign convert Hoffa from invincible union boss into a convicted felon—and how did Hoffa’s post-conviction “comedy troupe” tactics, the 1967 transfer of day-to-day power to Fitzsimmons, and Sheeran’s first experience of Fitz’s coldness set the conditions for later dual-loyalty crisis?

## Leading propositions

- Hoffa delivered the Master Freight Agreement in 1963 despite grand juries, rebel Voice, and Philadelphia resistance (p. 127).
- Philadelphia was the hardest Master Freight nut; defeating Voice and owning Cohen’s loyalty mattered as much as pay/pension rhetoric (pp. 127–128).
- May 9, 1963: Hoffa indicted Nashville jury tampering; June 4 same afternoon: Chicago Sun Valley pension indictment (p. 128).
- Sheeran stages a laxative/coffee-urn disruption of Arlington management negotiators to “thin out the herd” (pp. 128–129).
- Frank Ragano claims Hoffa ordered Marcello/Trafficante to kill JFK; Sheeran is skeptical of messenger, words, and tradecraft but “goes with” Ragano’s memory (pp. 129–130).
- Valachi hearings (Sept. 1963) open public war on Cosa Nostra and intensify scrutiny of Hoffa’s world (pp. 130–132).
- Days before Nov. 22, 1963, Sheeran couriered three rifles (duffel) from Tony Pro / Monte’s Brooklyn via Dave Ferrie at Campbell’s Cement, Baltimore (pp. 132–133).
- On JFK funeral day Hoffa blasts Osborn’s disbarment and says “Bobby Kennedy is just another lawyer now” (p. 134).
- RFK privately kept mob/Castro possibilities open after Dallas; William Hundley: organized-crime program “just stopped” when the bullet hit (pp. 134–135).
- Chattanooga jury-tampering trial: Partin surprise witness; Hoffa convicted; eight years; Judge Wilson: “tampered… with the very soul of this nation” (pp. 137–141).
- Chicago pension fraud: handwriting trap on “J.R. Hoffa” lease; five consecutive years; total thirteen (pp. 144–146).
- Post-conviction “comedy troupe”: prostitute affidavits, Brennan brewery threat, Bud Nichols juror-phone bug claim with no phones in jury rooms (pp. 148–149).
- Supreme Court affirms Partin use; Warren dissents as “affront to… fairness” (p. 149).
- March 7, 1967: Hoffa enters Lewisburg as Inmate 33298-NE (p. 149).
- Sheeran first feels Fitz’s coldness: DeGeorge shootout, hang-up, no bail/expenses; Russell forces Fitz to pay $50k via dry spot (pp. 150–155).
- Gorey/Rita hall shooting: Sheeran denies Hoffa ordered it; delivers $25,000 for shooters’ lawyers on Hoffa’s “I made a promise” (pp. 150–151).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: Master Freight required a solidified union; rebels and Cohen-style sweetheart deals fragmented leverage. **Conclusion**: Violence, campaigning, and Cohen’s fall were functional means to one national contract, not mere personal vendetta (pp. 127–128).
- **Premises**: Partin reported jury tampering to non-lawyer Sheridan, not defense strategy to prosecutors; judge accepts government frame. **Conclusion**: Partin’s testimony survives exclusion motion and becomes the puppeteer proof against Hoffa (pp. 138–139).
- **Premises**: Hoffa fears tapes of what he told Partin; hedges instead of flat denial while bribery events are independently proved. **Conclusion**: Paranoia about surveillance, not only weak counsel, collapses his Chattanooga performance (pp. 139–140).
- **Premises**: Sun Valley pledge and scramble loans are real; government needs secret 22% trust signature; Hoffa denies ever signing “J.R. Hoffa” on legal papers and is shown a Blair House lease he signed that way. **Conclusion**: Case turns on signature authenticity and personal-profit motive as much as on raw misuse of funds (pp. 145–146).
- **Premises**: After conviction, Partin must stay alive for recanting affidavits/pardon narrative; legal motions become theater. **Conclusion**: Brandt labels the campaign a “comedy troupe”—desperate improvisation after two losses rather than a serious path to freedom (pp. 141–149).
- **Premises**: Hoffa creates general VP, keeps salary in prison, banks legal fees, installs Fitz as puppet. **Conclusion**: He intends rule-from-jail continuity; Sheeran’s front-line experience will show Fitz is not a puppet (pp. 148, 150–155).
- **Premises**: Sheeran fires 107 disruptors on Hoffa’s orders; Fitz later orders him to “get things under control” at Spring Garden; after “One down. Two limping,” Fitz hangs up and denies support. **Conclusion**: Sheeran is first Hoffa man to feel the new wind—Fitz will not cover violence the way Hoffa would (pp. 152–155).
- **Premises**: Sheeran couriered rifles to Ferrie shortly before Dallas; Ragano claims verbal hit order; Hoffa gloats RFK is “just another lawyer.” **Conclusion**: Book builds circumstantial mob-JFK atmosphere while Sheeran denies hard knowledge and cannot ask (pp. 129–134). *(History note: high-stakes claim rests on Sheeran memory + Ragano memoir; not independently proved in this unit.)*

## Evidence offered

**Documentary / public-record style (Brandt narrative voice)**
- Nashville Test Fleet mistrial, Judge Miller’s grand-jury order on jury contact (p. 126).
- Indictment dates: May 9, 1963 jury tampering; June 4, 1963 Sun Valley; Cohen embezzlement conviction same day (p. 128).
- Valachi McClellan appearance September 1963; RFK commission testimony (pp. 131–132).
- Osborn tape, Rule to Show Cause, disbarment Nov. 20, 1963; venue shift to Chattanooga (pp. 132–134).
- Chattanooga trial structure: Neal’s cases against accomplices; Partin last; four-hour exclusion hearing; five-day cross (pp. 137–139).
- Verdicts/sentences: Hoffa eight years March 12, 1964; codefendants three years; Osborn 3½; Schiffer contempt sixty days; Ragano reprimand (pp. 140–141).
- Judge Frank W. Wilson sentencing language on “soul of this nation” (p. 141).
- Chicago: April 27, 1964 start; 13 weeks; 15,000+ documents; July 26, 1964 guilty; August 17 five consecutive years (pp. 144–146).
- RFK resigns AG Aug. 25, 1964; 201 Teamster officials indicted, 126 convicted (pp. 146–147).
- La Stella arrests Sept. 22, 1966 (p. 147).
- 1966 Miami Beach convention: general VP, salary-in-prison, $1,277,680 past legal fees (p. 148).
- Supreme Court Partin issue; brewery threat to Brennan’s brother; Warren dissent (p. 149).
- Lewisburg entry March 7, 1967; *Life* March 17 photo essay (p. 149).
- Mitchell affidavit later appears in next unit; here: Audie Murphy / Partin deposition claim in Hoffa autobiography (pp. 141–142).

- Rifle courier: “Go see your friend” → Russell → Monte’s / Tony Pro duffel → Ferrie in Baltimore (pp. 132–133).

- Fitz hang-up; Russell forces $35k + $15k Schiffer package = $50k (pp. 151–152, 154).

**Secondary / attributed**
- Ragano *Mob Lawyer* on “Booby” gin talk and July 23, 1963 kill-Kennedy order (pp. 129–130).
- Schlesinger / Goodwin on RFK’s post-Dallas suspicions (pp. 134–135).
- Hundley on OC program stopping (p. 135).
- Sheridan on pension-fund fraud meaning and Supreme Court oral argument uncertainty (pp. 146, 148).
- Giancana Greenbaum / Action Jackson examples of sadism (Brandt historical color) (pp. 135–136).

**Evidence quality notes (history genre)**
- Court outcomes, sentences, indictment dates, and Valachi publicity are standard public history and relatively strong.
- Partin’s status as “volunteer” vs. planted agent is a litigated legal construction the book reports as accepted by the trial judge—not re-litigated with fresh primary exhibits here.
- Ragano JFK-order claim is third-party memoir; Sheeran’s partial acceptance is hedged (“nobody in their right mind talks…”).
- Rifle-to-Ferrie story is pure Sheeran; no contemporaneous document in this unit. Causal link to Dallas is inference Sheeran himself treats as unaskable.
- Comedy-troupe episodes rely heavily on Walter Sheridan’s account (adversary source).
- Spring Garden / DeGeorge: Sheeran denies giving a shoot signal; no guns found; charges linger 1967–72 and are dismissed—narrative of self-exculpation under murder exposure.

## Open questions

- Did Hoffa actually give Ragano a kill-Kennedy message, or is that memoir embroidery Sheeran half-accepts for narrative completeness?
- What exactly was in the Monte’s duffel, and does any non-Sheeran source place him with Ferrie/Pro on that run?
- How much of the “comedy troupe” material is Sheridan’s spin versus verifiable court filings (Monday perjury, Nichols affidavit, Brennan approach)?
- Was Gorey’s killing Voice politics, gambling debt, or freelanced downtown violence—and why did Hoffa fund the defense if uninvolved?
- Did Fitz hang up from panic, cowardice, or deliberate distance from a homicide scene he had authorized with coded language?
- To what extent was Fitz ever a true “puppet,” and when did Commission preference for a weak president begin?
- How should readers weigh Sheeran’s self-portrait in the DeGeorge shootout (no signal, chaos, no guns) against D.A. Specter’s murder theory?
