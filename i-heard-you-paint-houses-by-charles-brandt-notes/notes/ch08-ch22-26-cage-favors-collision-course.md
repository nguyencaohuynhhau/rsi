# ch08: Cage, favors, collision course (pp. 156–195)

## Question this chapter answers

How did Hoffa’s prison years, the Fitzsimmons/Nixon/Mitchell “Get Hoffa Out” double game, and Sheeran’s dual role as Hoffa loyalist and Bufalino shooter put Hoffa on a collision course with the Commission—culminating in “They wouldn’t dare” / “all hell will break loose”?

## Leading propositions

- Lewisburg rules block union visitors; lawyers (Ragano, Shenker, Bill Bufalino, son James) become message channel; Sheeran’s “job interviews” create side-channel meetings (pp. 157–158).
- Early on, Russ/Fitz/Carlos/Santo try to free Hoffa; shotgun into Dorfman’s Cadillac is a message job, read as pressure on weak Fitz over pension loans (pp. 159–160).
- Hoffa–Tony Pro prison break over pension eligibility becomes permanent mutual hatred; Bill Bufalino resigns from Hoffa’s personal service (pp. 161–162).
- Parole denials 1969 and March 1971; Fitz announces he will run if Hoffa not paroled—direct challenge (pp. 162–163).
- Sheeran delivers ~$500k to Mitchell (May 1971); Pro/Fitz allegedly send competing half-million for a restricted parole (pp. 165–166).
- Hoffa resigns all union offices and endorses Fitz; Nixon appears at executive board; parole board still denies August 1971 (pp. 166–168).
- Dec. 23, 1971 commutation: Hoffa free for Christmas but barred from labor management until March 6, 1980—the final double-cross relative to Lewisburg draft papers Sheeran read (pp. 169–170).
- Hoffa plays possum until off paper March 1973; then sues restriction and announces 1976 challenge to Fitz (pp. 170–171, 183–184).
- Sheeran claims Crazy Joey Gallo hit at Umberto’s for Russell (protocol answer to Colombo public hit) (pp. 173–178).
- 1972 Delaware Senate: informational picket blocks Boggs anti-Biden inserts; papers undelivered; Biden wins (pp. 181–183).
- Mitchell affidavit (Oct. 15, 1973) after $270k drop denies DOJ initiated restriction—later tainted by Mitchell’s Watergate perjury (pp. 185–186).
- Hoffa lawsuit (Boudin) loses fact round before Judge Pratt July 1974; appeal pending into 1975 (pp. 186–188).
- Local 299 war: boat/car bombs, hang-ups; Hoffa puffs exposing mob loans and racketeers in Fitz’s Teamsters (pp. 190–191).
- Oct. 18, 1974 Frank Sheeran Appreciation Night + private Broadway Eddie’s meeting: Russell/Angelo tell Hoffa not to run; “failure to show appreciation… for Dallas”; Sheeran ordered to “tell him what it is” (pp. 188–195).
- Hoffa refuses: “It’s my union”; threatens media dumps; links Baltimore rifle package to Dallas; “all hell will break loose” (p. 195).
- Fat Tony Salerno earlier “would not stand in Jimmy’s way”; Commission mood shifts as Hoffa’s campaign style, not mere candidacy, costs support (pp. 189–190, 194).

## Arguments

- **Premises**: Prison cuts Hoffa’s direct control; Fitz enjoys the job and is soft on pension security; Pro is permanently alienated. **Conclusion**: Rule-from-jail becomes “pacing”—power erodes while hatred concentrates on Fitz and Pro (pp. 159–162).
- **Premises**: Dual cash to Mitchell; resignations sold as “new evidence” for parole; board still rejects and restriction appears only on final papers. **Conclusion**: Nixon–Mitchell–Fitzsimmons played both ends; Hoffa paid for freedom that structurally locked him out until 1980 (pp. 165–170).
- **Premises**: Constitutional attack on conditional pardon is strong in lawyers’ eyes; Hoffa waits until off paper then fights in public and court. **Conclusion**: Legal path is real but slow; Hoffa’s temperament will not let him “coast” (pp. 170–171, 183, 188).
- **Premises**: Sheeran remains Hoffa’s man (endorsement, drops, banquet) while remaining Russell’s shooter (Gallo) and courier (Mitchell). **Conclusion**: Dual loyalty is structurally stable only while Hoffa and Commission goals align; Broadway Eddie’s ends the alignment (pp. 172–179, 193–195).
- **Premises**: Hoffa publicly threatens to call bad loans, expose underworld in the union, defy restriction if courts fail. **Conclusion**: Russell hears “puffing” as dangerous publicity—same pattern that doomed Colombo/Gallo/Apalachin attention (pp. 190–194).
- **Premises**: Russell speaks for “people higher up”; soft “Dallas” debt; “If they could take out the president, they could take out the president of the Teamsters.” **Conclusion**: Deterrence flips: Hoffa’s “they wouldn’t dare / all hell will break loose” meets Commission capacity claim—collision course locked (pp. 194–195).
- **Premises**: Gallo hit follows Colombo public-hit protocol breach; Irish non-Italian shooters reduce family-to-family war risk. **Conclusion**: Sheeran positions himself as Commission instrument via Russell’s “liberal” non-Italian assets (pp. 173–179). *(Claim source: Sheeran only; contested by multi-shooter public stories he rejects.)*

- Clemency restriction clause quoted (p. 170).

**Evidence quality notes (history genre)**
- Parole/commutation dates, restriction language, lawsuit outcome, Nixon resignation, and Brill pension-fund scale are strong public-history anchors.
- Half-million and $270k Mitchell drops: Sheeran-only delivery claims; CREEP-era corruption is historically real, but these specific transfers are uncorroborated in-unit except Allen’s smaller satchel via 302.
- Competing Pro/Fitz half-million for restricted parole: Sheeran hears later; Russell “didn’t even know”—hearsay chain.
- Gallo murder: Sheeran claims sole shooter against multi-shooter lore; driver dead of cancer with alleged delirious implication of Sheeran in fourteen hits—classic untestable confession problem.
- Biden picket story: Sheeran admits written allegations exist; claims no knowledge whether Biden knew—political dynamite, single-source.
- “Dallas” lip-read line and Hoffa’s JFK roster are the book’s highest-stakes interpretive leap: build on ch07 rifle courier; still no hard document. Treat as *claimed speech acts* inside a confessional narrative, not established fact.
- Pratt holding is real legal reasoning (motives of signatory don’t void act); Hoffa’s expected appellate win remains aspirational inside this unit (decision falls after July 1975 disappearance timeline in later chapters).

## Open questions

- Did Hoffa knowingly accept a 1980 restriction in any oral deal Mitchell/Dean describe, or was the Lewisburg draft he and Sheeran celebrated deliberately swapped?
- How much cash, if any, can be independently traced from Hoffa/Dorfman/Vegas skim to Mitchell/Nixon beyond Allen’s contested $40k story?
- Was Fat Tony’s “not stand in the way” ever a firm Genovese position, or did Pro’s war and Hoffa’s publicity reverse it before 1975?
- Is the Broadway Eddie’s “Dallas” line Sheeran’s late confessional synthesis or a contemporaneous Russell speech act?
- Would Hoffa’s appellate constitutional theory have succeeded in late 1975 had he lived—and did the Commission act on *certainty* of his return or on *risk* plus loose talk?
- How should the Gallo confession be weighed against multi-shooter accounts and the dying Redhead’s alleged fourteen-hit list?
- When Sheeran says he told Russell Hoffa was “only puffing,” was he managing Russell, self-deceiving, or both—and when did he know the hit would fall to him?
