# ch02: Chapters 1–3 — “They Wouldn’t Dare” / What It Is / Get Yourself Another Punching Bag (pp. 6–23)

## Question this chapter answers

What chain of events in late July 1975, on Sheeran’s telling, turned a “peace mission” to Jimmy Hoffa into orders for his elimination—and what childhood and code formed the man who could receive those orders without showing anything in his face?

## Leading propositions
- Sheeran’s final hospital endorsement: he stood behind all the material in the book, including his role in what happened to Hoffa on July 30, 1975. (p. 5)
- Brandt places Sheeran’s words in the “court of public opinion,” to be judged as history by the reader. (p. 6)
- Brandt ends the prologue as pallbearer for Sheeran’s flag-draped green coffin and calls the book “the final chapter of the Hoffa tragedy.” (p. 6)
- Portions in Sheeran’s voice are marked by quote marks; Brandt’s sections add “critical detail and background information.” (p. 6)
- Sheeran claims he called Hoffa Sunday, July 27, 1975, from his own Philly phone as a “peace mission” authorized by Bufalino—not yet “in on the thing.” (pp. 6–7)
- After Nixon’s 1971 pardon, Hoffa became reckless, publicly threatening to expose the mob and cut off pension-fund access—hypocritical, Sheeran says, because Hoffa brought the mob in. (p. 7)
- At Frank Sheeran Appreciation Night, Hoffa gave Sheeran a diamond-encircled gold watch; Bufalino gave a special three-person gold-piece ring (Bufalino, underboss, Sheeran). (pp. 7–8)
- Bufalino was boss of a family based outside NYC but consulted by all families; briefly acting head of Anastasia’s family after the barber-chair hit. (p. 8)
- Night before the testimonial, Bufalino told Hoffa flat-out to stop running for Teamsters president; “certain people” preferred weak Fitzsimmons and easy pension loans. (pp. 8–9)
- Bufalino told Sheeran: “Talk to your friend. Tell him what it is”—which Sheeran treats as a death threat. (p. 9)
- Hoffa’s response: “They wouldn’t dare.” (p. 9)
- On the July 27 call, Sheeran floated a post-wedding sit-down at the lake; Hoffa agreed for Saturday ~12:30, “Just the two of you… Not the little guy.” (pp. 12–14)
- Sheeran cites Sam Giancana’s murder (kitchen, back of head, under chin “Sicilian style”) as warning after Time linked Bufalino and Giancana to CIA/Castro plots. (p. 12)
- Hoffa threatened that if anything “unnatural” happened to him, his records would come out. (p. 12)
- Monday July 28, Hoffa called again: he would meet Tony Pro Wednesday at the Red Fox, set up by Tony Jack; Sheeran was asked to be there at 2:00 with a gun. (pp. 14–15)
- Tony Jack Giacalone was close to Hoffa’s family but his wife was first cousin to Tony Pro—“serious with the Italians.” (p. 15)
- Sheeran told Bufalino immediately; later cannot recall Bufalino saying anything. (p. 15)
- Monday dinner at Brutico’s: Bufalino changes plans—stay until Wednesday morning; Sheeran reads this as keeping him away so Hoffa would be alone. (pp. 16–17)
- Bufalino: “Your friend was too late. There’s no need for you and me to meet him on Saturday by the lake.” (p. 17)
- Sheeran concludes he was spared “out of respect for Russell,” not because the plot needed him yet. (p. 18)
- Bufalino: “There won’t be a body.” Then, grinding with his thumb: “Dust to dust.” (p. 19)
- Bufalino assigns Sheeran a “quick fly over the lake and do a little errand in Detroit”—the opening of Sheeran’s claimed operational role. (p. 19)
- Sheeran was not born into the Mafia; Irish Catholic Philadelphian; “before I came home from the war I never did anything really wrong.” (p. 19)
- Depression childhood: theft of farm produce under birdshot; father’s steelwork and speakeasy fight bets; Catholic confession theology; altar-boy wine incident; father boxing him as punishment while brother Tom was spared. (pp. 20–23)
- Childhood Italian neighborhood and wartime Italian language later made mob figures trust him. (pp. 22–23)

## Arguments
- **Premises (Sheeran’s timeline)**: (1) Own-phone call Sunday = not yet conspirator; (2) Bufalino-authorized “peace” sit-down Saturday; (3) Hoffa then sets Wednesday Red Fox meet with Pro and asks Sheeran as armed backup; (4) Bufalino delays departure so Sheeran cannot cover Hoffa; (5) “no body” and “errand” follow. **Conclusion**: Sheeran presents himself as last-minute converter from peacemaker to participant under orders he could not refuse.
- **Premises**: Hoffa’s public anti-mob rhetoric + pension threats + “records” blackmail + Giancana example. **Conclusion** (shared Sheeran/Bufalino framing): Hoffa “made one threat too many”; the Commission could no longer tolerate him.
- **Premises**: Dual loyalty—Hoffa and Bufalino as the “two greatest men”; ring and watch still worn; “wrong look… and my house gets painted.” **Conclusion**: Survival required silence and obedience to Bufalino over friendship with Hoffa.
- **Premises (Brandt’s structural choice)**: Open on the week of the hit, then flash back to childhood. **Conclusion**: The crime is the book’s narrative center; biography explains the instrument, not the other way around.
- **Premises (Chapter Three origin story)**: Poverty, paternal violence, Catholic sin/absolution, fighting for place among Italians. **Conclusion**: Sheeran argues a continuous line from Depression “punching bag” to wartime and then mob enforcer—not innate Mafia birthright.

## Evidence offered
- **Sheeran oral narrative (primary, uncorroborated here)**: Full July 27–28 phone dialogues; Broadway Eddie’s / Warwick Hotel sit-downs; Brutico’s whispered orders; Red Fox logistics. (pp. 6–19)
- **Phone records claim**: “The FBI has a record of all these long-distance calls back and forth.” (p. 14)—offered as external check on the call pattern, not on content.
- **Public/contextual anchors named by Sheeran**:
  - Nixon pardon (1971); Fitzsimmons as caretaker president. (pp. 7–8)
  - Albert Anastasia barber-chair murder (Bufalino as acting head). (p. 8)
  - Sam Giancana hit; Time magazine CIA/Bay of Pigs/Castro plot reporting; Senate subpoena. (p. 12)
  - Jack Ruby / Havana / Judith Campbell / JFK–RFK strand (Sheeran’s cocktail history). (p. 12)
  - Tony Pro prison beef, Miami sit-down threats, later life sentence for the “bad hit” (Sally Bugs / K.O. Konigsberg nylon rope). (pp. 13–14)
  - Tony Jack death Feb. 2001; headline “Reputed U.S. Mobster Takes Hoffa Secret to Grave.” (p. 15)
  - Lincoln Continental later used in 1981 labor-racketeering case against Sheeran. (p. 16)
  - Russell’s quit-smoking bet with Jimmy Blue Eyes after Castro expelled them from Cuba (1960). (p. 16)
- **Objects as memory anchors**: diamond watch (Hoffa), three-dollar gold-piece ring (Bufalino), daughters’ birthstone ring. (p. 8)
- **Yellow pad link**: Sheeran ends Chapter One noting Jimmy would sit with “Russ & Frank” “with our names on that yellow pad.” (p. 14)—ties prologue artifact to this week’s plan.
- **Bias / source posture**: Almost all load-bearing claims are Sheeran’s first-person reconstruction decades later; Brandt’s role in these chapters is mainly chapter titles, Author’s Note dual-voice rule, and the pallbearer coda—minimal independent corroboration inside this unit.

## Tensions / links to other chapters
- **Prologue (ch01) ↔ Chapter One**: “Russ & Frank” yellow pad becomes the planned Saturday lake sit-down that never happens; James P.’s “would get in a car with him” foreshadows Sheeran as the only viable lure—here still cast as backup, not yet shooter.
- **Peace mission vs. errand**: Chapter One builds Sheeran as reluctant peacemaker; Chapter Two ends with assignment of the Detroit “errand”—the book’s central moral hinge, to be paid off in ch09 (July 30).
- **“They wouldn’t dare” vs. “what it is”**: Hoffa’s bravado title-phrase collides with Bufalino’s soft-spoken absolute; Giancana is the proof of concept inside this unit.
- **Dual greatest men**: Hoffa and Bufalino both honored Sheeran; ring vs. watch symbolize split sovereignty—conflict that structures the whole book.
- **Code lexicon** (Australia, little brother, paint houses, what it is) will recur as the book’s operational language.
- **Childhood → war → mob**: Chapter Three opens the origin arc continued in ch03 (WWII “university”); confession theology links back to monsignor absolution and deathbed confession frame (ch01).
- **Reliability flags inside Sheeran’s own story**: Claims he was not “in on it” Sunday (own phone); cannot recall Bufalino’s reaction to the Red Fox news; later becomes the errand-runner—readers must watch whether later chapters revise this “outsider then insider” timeline.
- **Tony Pro / Tony Jack / Red Fox**: Plants the official public meeting narrative of July 30 against Sheeran’s private claim that the real decision was already made Monday night at Brutico’s.
